01 / Exterior

Read the house before you step inside.

Rex begins with the exterior because the outside tells the truth first. Roof geometry, grade slope, siding transitions, window conditions, and weather exposure all show how the house is aging and where risk may be collecting.

  • Roofline and surface transitions are scanned for wear, flashing detail, and drainage direction.
  • Openings and trim are checked for signs of failed sealants and weather entry.
  • Site slope and surface runoff are read before interior staining ever becomes visible.

02 / Moisture

Water movement is often the first hidden story.

Drainage is one of the clearest examples of why inspection matters. A home can look perfectly maintained and still be working against itself if runoff is pooling near the structure, if grading is wrong, or if moisture is being trapped where it should be shed.

  • Low points, downspout discharge, and runoff paths matter before interior symptoms appear.
  • Moisture usually leaves subtle exterior evidence before it becomes expensive interior repair.
  • Exterior grading and water control shape long-term foundation and crawlspace behavior.

03 / Roof

The roof is not a checkbox. It is a risk map.

Shingles, penetrations, flashing, visible wear, and ventilation clues all help Rex assess how well the top of the home is doing its job. Buyers rarely get a second chance on roof understanding after closing.

  • Penetrations, valleys, and transitions often reveal the most important clues.
  • Surface wear can suggest remaining life, deferred maintenance, or recent patchwork.
  • Roof and attic conditions have ripple effects on insulation, moisture, and comfort.

04 / Structure

Movement matters when the pattern starts making sense.

One crack by itself means very little. A pattern of movement, slope, separation, or repeated signs across the property means more. Rex reads visible structure clues in context so clients understand what is cosmetic, what deserves monitoring, and what should be evaluated further.

  • Settlement indicators are read as patterns, not isolated moments.
  • Openings, trim lines, and slab behavior often help tell the larger structural story.
  • Clients need calm interpretation, not dramatic wording.

05 / Systems

Visible systems clues change the ownership picture fast.

Electrical panels, HVAC equipment, plumbing fixtures, and visible system performance all contribute to the real cost of a house. A clean cosmetic presentation means very little if the core systems are aging poorly or showing maintenance gaps.

  • Mechanical age and visible condition affect repair timing and budget.
  • Electrical and plumbing clues often point to upgrade needs or deferred service.
  • Function matters as much as appearance once you own the property.
