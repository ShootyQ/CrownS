01 / Exterior

Start outside. The house will usually tell on itself.

Before you ever fool with the inside, the outside is already talking. Roof shape, siding lines, window trim, and how the ground falls away from the house can tell you a whole lot about what kind of money and trouble may be waiting.

  • Roof edges and transitions get checked for wear, flashing trouble, and where water wants to run.
  • Window and trim details can show where weather has been trying to sneak in.
  • Ground slope matters more than folks think when water starts hanging around the foundation.

02 / Moisture

Water is patient. It will keep after a weak spot all year long.

Drainage is one of the biggest money subjects on a house, and folks miss it all the time. A place can look sharp on showing day and still be shoving water right back toward the foundation every time it rains.

  • Low spots, downspout discharge, and runoff paths matter before the drywall ever stains.
  • Wet soil and bad splash control are small signs that can lead to bigger repair bills.
  • Good drainage can save a house a lot of grief over the years.

03 / Roof

The roof is not a line item. It is a whole story by itself.

Shingles, boots, flashing, penetrations, and attic airflow all matter. If the roof is getting tired or the details were done sloppy, the rest of the house may wind up paying for it later.

  • Pipes, valleys, and flashing details usually show trouble before anything else does.
  • Wear patterns on shingles help tell whether you are looking at age, neglect, or patchwork.
  • Attic conditions back up what the roof is doing and how the house is breathing.

04 / Structure

A crack by itself may not mean much. A pattern is where it gets serious.

Rex is not there to spook anybody. He is there to sort out what looks like plain settling and what may point toward movement, stress, or repair work that deserves a closer look before money changes hands.

  • One line crack is one thing. Repeating cracks and shifted openings are another.
  • Doors, trim, wall lines, and foundation behavior all help tell the bigger story.
  • Clients need a steady read, not somebody trying to put on a show.

05 / Systems

The pretty parts do not run the house. The systems do.

Panels, water heaters, outlets, visible plumbing, and equipment age all shape what owning the place is really going to feel like. A home can look mighty clean and still have plenty of catching up to do where it counts.

  • Equipment age and condition usually tell you what kind of repair budget is coming.
  • Electrical details can point toward safety needs, updates, or plain old neglect.
  • Once the keys are yours, function matters more than fresh paint ever did.
